Akkodis·Internal project·2023

Chain²Bike dApp

A decentralized app making e-bike ownership transparent, with a blockchain-based tamper-proof digital logbook and secure peer-to-peer ownership transfers.

My role

User flows·Design system·Logo·UI design

Topic

Blockchain·Trust & Security

Overview

01Problem

No trust in used e-bikes

Buying or selling a used e-bike comes with uncertainty: no verified history of ownership, maintenance or accidents. The real challenge was making blockchain technology accessible to everyday users, communicating security and trust without technical jargon.

02Approach

Highly technical processes made easy

Keep the technology in the background: three core flows, registering a bike, keeping the digital logbook and transferring ownership, built on familiar and trustworthy interactions instead of technical jargon.

03Result

A tamper-proof logbook in your pocket

The dApp shows any registered e-bike’s history, from previous owners to workshop visits, and ownership transfers happen directly between parties, without middlemen. After further iterations, the prototype became a reference project for internal innovation at Akkodis.

Main decisions

01

Keep the complex technology invisible

Users should experience security and simplicity first, so the design avoids technical jargon and relies on clarity, neutral tones and clean typography.

02

Only three core flows

Registering a bike, the digital logbook and the ownership transfer cover what buyers and sellers actually need, and keep the blockchain complexity out of sight.

Full process

Initial user research and journey mapping was handled by a colleague. I onboarded with a thorough review of the findings and the blockchain architecture to understand both user needs and the technical constraints shaping the design space.

We defined the three core user flows: registering a bike, viewing and maintaining the digital logbook, and completing an ownership transfer. The design challenge was abstracting blockchain complexity into familiar, trustworthy interactions.

We created the Chain²Bike logo, a modular design system, and all interface screens. The visual language based on the AKKODIS CI prioritises clarity and trust, using neutral tones and clean typography to communicate security without intimidating non-technical users.

A clickable prototype was developed and evaluated internally. Feedback focused on the ownership transfer flow and the readability of the logbook, and both were refined based on the results to achieve the clearest possible experience.
