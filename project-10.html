Personal project·2026

Voteasy Votecounter

A mobile-first concept for AI-assisted vote counting on election night, designed to work within Germany's existing paper ballot system.

My role

Field observation·Interviews·Concept·Flow design

Topic

Civic Tech·Digital Elections

Overview

01Problem

No time to learn a complex system

At a local election in 2025, I worked as a volunteer poll worker and experienced the newly introduced digital counting system firsthand: unclear terminology, buried actions and a flow slower than pen and paper. For a tool used once a year, under time pressure and with no training, that is not acceptable.

02Approach

ASSAP: As simple & safe as possible

Keep the existing system, including the paper ballot, and make only the counting digital. AI helps read the ballots, but whenever it is unsure, a person decides; in the background, it also checks that the counted results match up. Poll workers, vote counters and the municipal administration share one flow, yet none of them depends on another to get their part done.

03Result

Ready to pitch

A concept and prototype that volunteers can use without any training. Fellow poll workers who reviewed it responded immediately: clear, fast, and something they would use that same night. The next step is pitching it to the responsible municipal office.

Main decisions

01

Keep the paper ballot

In Germany, paper ballots deliberately include voters who cannot or should not have to use digital systems. Only the counting becomes digital, the voter experience stays unchanged.

02

QR code instead of logins

Access works via QR code, so volunteers need no account and no training.

03

AI suggests, people confirm

AI reads the ballot photo and suggests a result, and the human confirms or corrects it: quicker than counting by hand, and safer because a person always has the final say.

Full process

I spoke with fellow poll workers to check whether my experience was shared. It was. Recurring themes: unfamiliar terminology, no clear flow, and a system that assumed expert knowledge. The core insight: this tool is used once a year, under time pressure, by volunteers with no prior training. It has to work immediately.

A fully digital election would make this unnecessary. But in Germany, paper ballots remain a deliberate choice to include voters who cannot or should not have to interact with digital systems. Voteasy Votecounter works within that reality: it digitises the counting process behind the scenes, leaving the voter experience completely unchanged.

The concept connects three roles: poll worker at the entrance, vote counter at the table, and municipal administration. Each sees only what they need. Access works via QR code, no login or training required. AI reads the ballot photo and suggests a result; the human confirms or corrects. All labels use the terminology poll workers already know.
