Linköping University·Course project·2024

Little Food Fighters

A third of all food is wasted, and there are plenty of opportunities to reduce it. We developed a playful way to empower kids with the knowledge to do so, produced sustainably from what already exists.

My role

Character design·Illustration·Print design·AR animation

Topic

Game Design·Sustainability

Overview

01Problem

Too much of our food goes to waste

One third of all food produced globally is wasted, and changing that starts at home. Everyday life offers so many small opportunities to reduce it, and by making each other aware of them, we can make a big difference together.

02Approach

Simple, playful, sustainable

We wanted to reach even the youngest with something that empowers instead of lectures: no extra resources or money needed, no hurdle to get started, accessible right away and learned through play.

03Result

Learning in the bag

A cooperative board game printed on a standard ICA grocery bag, with an AR companion app that brings the food characters to life. Players rescue expiring food from the waste bin, and the game reaches families at the moment food decisions are made. Faculty saw SDG 12 addressed in a concrete, age-appropriate way, and instructors wanted to see it in actual supermarkets.

Main decisions

01

The grocery bag as the board

No extra purchase or materials needed, and the game reaches families exactly when they shop.

02

Cooperative, not competitive

Players solve shared tasks together, including donating food to players in need. Sustainability is not explained, it is played.

03

AR as a reason to replay

During playtesting, the AR feature generated immediate excitement and became a strong motivator for repeat play.

Full process

We studied the everyday situations where food waste actually happens at home, and looked at how existing games tackle sustainability and what makes them engaging for kids. Most interventions target adults; games that worked for younger audiences relied on concrete tasks, cause-and-effect, and collaboration.

We identified the everyday moments where kids can actually help avoid food waste and built the game around those. We also explored how to keep the format itself sustainable, using only what already exists. Core mechanics: cooperative play, shared tasks including donating food to players in need, and a grocery bag format that puts the game directly in families' hands. Sustainability is not explained, it is played.

While teammates handled app development and task design, my focus was on character design, print layout, and the AR animation concept and visuals. Food characters were illustrated as colorful, gender and skin-tone neutral figures. The print layout was designed for standard ICA grocery bag packaging. The AR companion app brings characters to life when scanned.

The game was playtested with kids aged 7 to 10 from our personal circle. Observations focused on engagement, rule clarity, and whether the sustainability message landed naturally. The AR feature generated immediate excitement and became a strong motivator for repeat play.
