ELLA: Lifelong Learning with AI

An international EU research project investigating how self-regulated learning can be sustainably embedded in everyday classroom instruction.

My role

Stakeholder onboarding·Workshop facilitation·Research synthesis·Personas

Topic

International Research Project·Self-Regulated Learning

Overview

01Problem

Stuck in the theory

Teachers across Europe want to bring self-regulated learning into the classroom, but most have no time to read into the methods, or to apply them. Students, in turn, are rarely even aware of SRL as a concept. Individual support stays out of reach for both sides.

02Approach

Structure shapes awareness

We focussed on how self-regulated learning fits into curricula, existing systems and different types of lessons. A clear structure builds awareness of SRL step by step, so the methods can be implemented smoothly in everyday teaching, without extra work. Learning experts and stakeholders were part of the design process from the start.

03Result

Built for real classrooms

When I left the project, the first prototype was ready for user tests with teachers and students, and the project is committed to long-term continuation. With €400,000 in EU funding and schools from multiple countries involved from day one, it is built for real deployment.

Main decisions

01

Finding out what stops teachers

Teachers already knew the principle of SRL and wanted to use it, but simply had no time for it. So the research question became what was actually stopping it in everyday lessons.

02

Bringing stakeholders into the design process

I introduced them to how we move from the problem space to the solution space, how a design cycle works and how users are involved in testing. Along the way, they learned to trust the process from discovery to design.

03

Experts as co-designers

In workshops, the experts uncovered problems with me, generated solutions and prioritised them. In regular feedback rounds, we evaluated interim results together, and they contributed ideas for further use cases and user groups.

Full process

Teachers repeatedly said they had no capacity for additional work. That became a research question in itself: not whether they wanted SRL, but what was actually stopping it. To find out, the ELLA team analysed 122 research papers, ran a student survey, and brought together 16 learning experts from across Europe. Students, meanwhile, had no framework for SRL at all.

Research findings were consolidated and prioritized in workshops with the expert group. We built personas for teachers and students and mapped their pain points along a learning session. After exploring the real needs behind, I guided the ideation session with the expert group and later dived deeper into the concept.

Solution approaches were evaluated in regular feedback rounds with the experts, who also contributed further ideas for different use cases and user groups. I created different schemas for better understanding how the solution fits into their curriculum, currently used systems, the different types of learning sessions, planning structures and how the students and teachers support play together.

The first prototype is currently being evaluated in user tests with teachers and students. The expert community and participating schools across different countries have responded positively, and the project is committed to long-term continuation.
